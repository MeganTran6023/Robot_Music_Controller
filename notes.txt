**RTOS Concepts**

* In your `vInteractionTask`,
you use both `vTaskDelay()` and `sleep_us()`.
Why did you choose to use `sleep_us()`
for the buzzer toggling,
but `vTaskDelay()`
for the joystick polling loop,
and how does this specific choice
affect the RTOS background processing?

A:

vTaskDelay - longer waits 
sleep_us - shorter waits (busy wait)

if you switch the two up, the whole system lock
- doesnt process the other action

ex)

total time is 500 ms

if task A runs for 10 ms, it waits for 490 ms -> use vTaskDelay

if task B runs for the remaining 490 ms -> use sleep_ms as it only waits
for 10 ms

**Task Scheduling**

* Your `vInteractionTask` (Priority 2)
has a higher priority than `vBlinkTask` (Priority 1).
Exactly how does the `vTaskDelay(pdMS_TO_TICKS(50))` call
inside your 2.5-second joystick polling loop
allow the lower-priority blink task to execute
without being starved?

1)The vInteractionTask does not use a semaphore lock during the 2.5-second window.

Instead, vTaskDelay(pdMS_TO_TICKS(50)) is called during every iteration of the polling loop.

This temporarily places the higher-priority vInteractionTask into a Blocked state for 50 milliseconds at a time.

While the interaction task is blocked, the FreeRTOS scheduler automatically assigns CPU time to the Ready, lower-priority vBlinkTask.

This allows the LCD face to update without being starved.
